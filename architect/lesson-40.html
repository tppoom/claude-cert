💡 แนวคิดหลักสำคัญ (Key Concept)

Inherited bias — A Claude-based system reproduces whatever its inputs treat as normal. That covers what the model was trained on, the examples in the prompt, the documents it retrieves and the cases it was tested against. Fairness only exists where someone measured it for each group a decision affects.

📄 ดูเนื้อหาและศัพท์ต้นฉบับภาษาอังกฤษ (Original English Reference) ▼

Ethical problems in an AI build rarely come from one bad decision. Usually something the system leaned on was skewed and nobody checked it. A shortlisting assistant, a loan-eligibility helper and an essay-feedback tool all turn inputs into judgements about people. If any of those inputs treats one kind of person as the default, the output will too. That is why bias, fairness and transparency are architecture concerns and not just policy text.

Training data is the first entry point. A model knows what it was exposed to during training and nothing else. Topics that appeared often and consistently are well covered. Rare, niche, local or contested topics sit at the edge. The practical question is how well represented a group or situation was in what the model read. Where coverage is thin, the model's sense of what is "normal" comes from whoever dominated its training data.

Prompts and examples are the second entry point, and this one is yours. Suppose every few-shot example of a strong candidate has the same kind of career path. The model learns that path as the template and treats other paths as weaker. Claude itself is cautious about content built on stereotypes, but a prompt can still teach a narrow default through what it chooses to show.

Retrieval corpora are the third. Retrieval, search and tools exist to fill gaps in the model's knowledge, but they also make your corpus part of what the model knows. A policy library that only covers head-office procedures gives confident, grounded answers to some employees and thin ones to everyone else. Evaluation choices are the last way in, and the easiest to miss. A test set built only from typical cases will pass a system that fails the people it never included.

2.Measuring fairness across groups and phrasings

สาระสำคัญและหลักการทำงานของหัวข้อนี้: หัวข้อนี้เจาะลึก Measuring fairness across groups and phrasings ตามมาตรฐานและพิมพ์เขียวการสอบของ Anthropic โดยมุ่งเน้นการทำความเข้าใจสถาปัตยกรรมเชิงลึก ข้อดีข้อเสียทางเทคนิค และแนวทางการปฏิบัติจริงในโปรดักชัน
⚠️ จุดที่ข้อสอบชอบหลอก (Exam Trap)

On the examWhen an answer pairs "standardise the prompt" with "re-test across variants", it fixes the cause and verifies the fix. Answers that only disclose, suppress or ration the output leave the cause unchanged.

⚠️ จุดที่ข้อสอบชอบหลอก (Exam Trap)

Exam trapAdding a disclaimer that AI explanations may vary between applicants deals with the fairness concern.Why is that wrong?A disclaimer leaves the behaviour that causes the inconsistency in place. The fix is to standardise the decision factors every explanation cites and re-test across phrasing variants, because sincere questions deserve substantive answers however they are worded.

คลิกดูเฉลยคำตอบพร้อมคำอธิบาย (Click to view answer & explanation) ▼

Standardising the decision factors fixes the prompt behaviour that causes the inconsistency, and re-testing across phrasing variants shows whether the fix holds. A disclaimer only describes the problem. Removing explanations takes away transparency, and rationing detail to those who ask writes the unequal treatment into policy.

“Tests make post-launch iteration safe. The test-first habit is why you can keep changing things confidently.”

กลไกการทำงานเบื้องหลัง (Under the Hood): ในหัวข้อ "Measuring fairness across groups and phrasings" หัวใจสำคัญตามแนวทางของ Anthropic คือการมองเห็นความแตกต่างระหว่าง Predefined Code Logic (Deterministic) กับ Model Autonomy (Stochastic) ระบบที่ควบคุมด้วยโค้ดจะให้ผลลัพธ์ที่คาดเดาได้ (Predictability) สูงกว่า ใช้โทเค็นน้อยกว่า และวัดผลได้ง่ายกว่าเสมอ

📄 ดูเนื้อหาและศัพท์ต้นฉบับภาษาอังกฤษ (Original English Reference) ▼

Fairness is a measurement, not a property you can assume. Leaving group names out of a prompt does not make the outputs fair. The only evidence is outcome metrics, such as approval rates, shortlist rates and error rates, compared group by group. In practice this is [reading results per segment](lesson:4.3), with demographic or user group as the segment. An overall accuracy figure can look fine while one group carries most of the errors.

How a person phrases a question is a group dimension too. Take a loan-eligibility assistant that gives a detailed decline rationale to an applicant who writes formally and a vague one to an applicant who writes casually, when the facts are the same. Applicants who already know how to phrase things well get better treatment. Claude's own guidelines set the bar: it treats sincere questions as deserving substantive answers whatever their wording. A decision-support product should meet the same bar.

The fix belongs in the design. Rewrite the explanation prompt so every decline rationale cites the same categories of decision factors, whatever the question looked like. Then re-test with a set of phrasing variants that ask for the same facts in different ways. Tests are what make that kind of change safe after launch, because they show whether the fix holds.

Three tempting shortcuts fail this check:

- Removing explanations altogether removes transparency along with the inconsistency, and declined applicants are left with nothing to understand or contest. - Giving full detail only to people who explicitly ask for it builds the same unequal treatment into policy. - A disclaimer that explanations "may vary" describes the problem and leaves the prompt behaviour that causes it in place.

3.Honest confidence and room for the user's own reasoning

สาระสำคัญและหลักการทำงานของหัวข้อนี้: หัวข้อนี้เจาะลึก Honest confidence and room for the user's own reasoning ตามมาตรฐานและพิมพ์เขียวการสอบของ Anthropic โดยมุ่งเน้นการทำความเข้าใจสถาปัตยกรรมเชิงลึก ข้อดีข้อเสียทางเทคนิค และแนวทางการปฏิบัติจริงในโปรดักชัน
⚠️ จุดที่ข้อสอบชอบหลอก (Exam Trap)

Transparency also covers how sure the system says it is. Anthropic trains Claude to be calibrated, meaning the confidence it expresses should match the evidence it has. Calibration matters most where the evidence is mixed. One example is a shortlisting rationale for a candidate whose qualifications are ambiguous. A calibrated rationale says the evidence is mixed and explains why. It does not state a firm verdict, and it does not lean toward the most favourable reading.

You can reinforce this in the prompt. Explicitly allowing Claude to say it does not have enough information to judge reduces confident false statements. That differs from returning a blank whenever a file is incomplete. A blank tells the recruiter nothing, while a stated uncertainty tells them where to look. Restricting rationales to direct résumé quotes doesn't produce calibration either. It just hides the judgement the recruiter needs to weigh.

The other half of honesty is not steering. In an essay-feedback tool, the student is supposed to build their own argument. An assistant that keeps nudging every essay toward one side of a debate topic undermines the student's epistemic autonomy, which is their right to reach conclusions through their own reasoning. Claude's guidelines describe the intended behaviour. It is cautious about giving personal opinions on contested topics, can decline to share them to avoid influencing people, and offers alternative perspectives so people can work through the question for themselves. Asking for the strongest case on one side is a request for that case, not for Claude's own view.
